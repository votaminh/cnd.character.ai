Large Language Models (LLMs) do NOT think like humans. GPT (Generative Pre-trained Transformer) based LLMs are glorified auto-text-complete programs (what should the next word be?). While it's correct to think of LLMs as a web of connections of varying strengths between words, it might be more helpful to imagine LLMs as webs of texts (news articles, Reddit comments, novels, etc) with connections to each other based on how many words they share and how often those texts appear or associate together. These pieces of text cluster together with text that have similar themes and ideas (news articles together, novels together with smaller clusters for specific genres, and so on) with a few weak connections between clusters. Given a specific input prompt the LLM will follow one of these connections to give a piece of text similar to your input. While one can roughly control where the LLM starts within their web of associated texts it's very hard to control where the LLM will travel within this web of associations. \n\n\nADVICE FOR WRITING CHARACTER PROMPTS: \nRestrictions are fiddly: telling an LLM \"do not do X\" makes it more likely the chatbot will actually do \"X\" because texts with \"X\" in it will associate more strongly with the sentence \"do not do X\". Avoid using the word \"not\" (use words like \"miserable\" instead of \"not happy\" because it has fewer associations to happy texts).
